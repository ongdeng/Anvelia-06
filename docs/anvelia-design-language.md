## Core Identity

Anvelia is quiet hillside hospitality expressed through Japanese-influenced thresholds, Malaysian nature, and exacting material detail. It is a resort first. Wellness is felt through fresh air, slower pace, shade, timber, greenery, water, and attentive hosting; it is not presented as a clinic, treatment program, or medical promise.

The emotional standard is **silent wealth**: quality is discovered through proportion, material, light, restraint, and care rather than announced through luxury language or decorative spectacle.

The experience should feel:

- quiet but not empty;
- premium but not showy;
- warm but not rustic;
- natural but not wild or unfinished;
- intimate but not small;
- Japanese-influenced but not themed or imitative;
- composed, observant, and unhurried.

## Threshold Principle

The central spatial idea is the threshold: outside to inside, light to shade, city pace to hillside pace. Express it through timber frames, architectural openings, layered foregrounds, distant views, paper beside forest-dark material, and alternating image/text compositions. Details must feel structural or tactile, never pasted on for decoration.

## Visual System

**Colour and material:** derive colour from warm paper, soft ivory, deep botanical green, charcoal, rain-darkened timber, muted stone, moss, and restrained aged-brass warmth. Avoid pure black, brilliant white, glossy gold, bright green, fashionable gradients, and digital-looking fades.

**Typography:** use a gentle editorial serif for the name and section titles, and a quiet neutral sans serif for body and practical information. Small uppercase labels are accents, not body copy. Preserve readable sizing, natural tracking, generous line height, and deliberate line breaks.

**Botanical work:** linework should resemble an archival drawing embedded in paper or pigment. It must remain subordinate to copy and architecture.

**Layout:** compose the page as a sequence of spatial chapters rather than repeated marketing blocks. Use controlled asymmetry, stable proportions, negative space, and one primary visual gesture per section. Do not force every section into an identical template.

**Interaction:** navigation must be stable and contact must remain convenient but composed. Use motion only for functional feedback or an exceptionally restrained reveal. Do not add decorative section-transition animation, scroll spectacle, parallax, or effects that delay access to content.

## Image Narrative

Every image needs a distinct job:

- **Hero:** arrival and revelation through a threshold.
- **Place:** altitude, landscape, air, and openness.
- **Cabins:** intimacy, warmth, material detail, and the desire to stay.
- **Open-Air Living:** tactile daily life, shade, timber, tea, water, and greenery.
- **Gatherings:** human-scale hosting, shared tables, and quiet connection.
- **Visit:** welcome, orientation, and a calm closing atmosphere.

Avoid repeated misty estate views, dramatic rain, fantasy architecture, generic property-advertising compositions, and stock-like lifestyle scenes. All current generated imagery is concept material unless independently verified; it must not prove exact buildings, facilities, people, services, views, weather, or capacity.

## Copy And Hospitality

Copy should be concise, sensory, assured, and specific without overclaiming. Describe what a guest may feel and notice before making operational claims. Avoid internal design explanations, wellness jargon, exaggerated superlatives, and repeated elevation or location facts. Anvelia welcomes contact through WhatsApp; Phase 1 contains no pricing, inquiry form, social links, or gallery.

## Responsive Expression

Landscape may use architectural split compositions and broader image relationships. Portrait should preserve the same identity through vertical chapter rhythm, carefully cropped images, readable type, and material continuity.

Approved full-screen narrative chapters use the shared `ViewportChapter` contract: exactly one stable `100svh` outer chapter in portrait and landscape. This standardizes pacing, not composition. Use section-specific image/text tracks and compact short-landscape spacing so all content remains visible; never clip, hide, or unnaturally compress copy to meet the height. New Phase 1 narrative sections should use this contract unless an explicit design review approves an exception.

## Decision Test

Before accepting any copy, image, component, or interaction, ask:

1. Does it feel calm, considered, and materially grounded?
2. Does it express hospitality rather than spectacle?
3. Is the detail structural, tactile, or useful rather than decorative filler?
4. Does the image perform a distinct narrative role?
5. Is the language truthful and free of unverified implications?
6. Does it feel quietly expensive rather than visibly luxurious?
7. Does it strengthen the original Option 2 threshold direction?

If the answer to any critical question is no, refine or remove the element.
